In the modern world, it is necessary for many businesses and organisations to collect user data in order to operate. However, the volume and sensitivity of this data may vary depending on what the specific data is. “Data fuels innovation; therefore, it is critical to have a framework that focuses on regulating how organizations obtain, process, and use data.” (VCE, 2024) Organisations are legally required to obtain consent from an end user before collecting their data. However, the ethics behind what data must be collected and what data must remain private are not always clear.

There are instances where the collection of private data could be considered as justified. For example, law enforcement may require sensitive data regarding an individual in order to stop or investigate crimes.(Santa Clara Univertisy, 2013) Personal Data is also useful in the Healthcare Sector. "Data collection in healthcare allows health systems to create holistic views of patients, personalize treatments, advance treatment methods, improve communication between doctors and patients, and enhance health outcomes."(Natallia Sakovich, No Date). The collection of certain personal information by healthcare organisations can be used to provide personalised healthcare at a huge scale 

 
Additionally, Users are often not aware what is being done with their data and what the intent behind its collection is, which leads them to be further uninformed. Such a case happened with Facebook in 2018, where a political analytics firm had used an external application to pay users to partake in a personality quiz. Unbeknownst to these users, their information and that of themselves was being used to create psychological profiles on users based on their liked posts and other sensitive information, such as their home addresses, and use this information for political campaigns. (University of Greater Manchester, 2021) The end users were giving uninformed consent, leading to their data being used in ways that had neither foreseen nor intended.
