In the modern world, it is necessary for many businesses and organisations to 	collect user data in order to operate. However, the volume and sensitivity of this data may vary depending on what the specific data is. “Data fuels innovation; therefore, it is critical to have a framework that focuses on regulating how organizations obtain, process, and use data.” (VCE, 2024) Organisations are legally required to obtain consent from an end user before collecting their data. However, many websites have exploited a loophole in this law. Explanations for what the user is consenting to can be long-winded ad overcomplicated, accompanied by options like “accept all” and “manage”, making it difficult to understand and leading to uninformed consent to cookies on websites (Annmarie Hanlon and Karen Jones, 2023) as it is unrealistic for a user browsing the internet to fully read the terms and conditions for every website they visit. 

Additionally, Users are often not aware what is being done with their data and what the intent behind its collection is, which leads them to be further uninformed. Such a case happened with Facebook in 2018, where a political analytics firm had used an external application to pay users to partake in a personality quiz. Unbeknownst to these users, their information and that of themselves was being used to create psychological profiles on users based on their liked posts and other sensitive information, such as their home addresses, and use this information for political campaigns. (University of Greater Manchester, 2021) Here, the end users were giving uninformed consent, leading to their data being used in ways that had neither foreseen nor intended.
